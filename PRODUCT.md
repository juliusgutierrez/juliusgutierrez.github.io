# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Decision-makers at businesses evaluating Julius Gutierrez for consulting or
contract engineering work: founders, engineering leads, and procurement-minded
stakeholders. They arrive skeptical and time-poor, often from a referral or a
LinkedIn link, and they are sizing up whether this person is senior enough to
trust with a real system. Their job to be done is a fast, confident judgment:
"Is this someone I'd hand a critical project to?"

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio site that establishes Julius as a senior software engineer
and system analyst with 10+ years modernizing enterprise systems. It is not a
lead-capture funnel; there is no hard conversion. Success is a strong, lasting
impression of seniority and credibility, such that the visitor leaves believing
he is a safe, capable choice and remembers him when work comes up.

## Brand Personality

Confident and precise. Calm authority over hype. Three words: assured,
exacting, grounded. The voice states facts and outcomes plainly and never
oversells. The interface should evoke trust and competence, the feeling of
working with someone who has shipped hard things and does not need to shout
about it.

## Anti-references

- **Generic dev-template portfolio.** The interchangeable dark-blue gradient
  with glowing cards that every bootcamp grad ships. This site must not be
  guessable as a template.
- **Cluttered resume dump.** Walls of text with every job detail exposed at
  once, no hierarchy, no breathing room. Depth should be available, not
  dumped.
- **Trendy but soulless.** Glassmorphism, neon-on-black, motion for motion's
  sake. Style with no substance reads as the opposite of senior.

## Design Principles

1. **Show seniority, do not claim it.** Let concrete outcomes, systems, and
   numbers carry the weight. Adjectives like "senior" or "expert" prove
   nothing; shipped work does.
2. **Restraint is the signal.** Calm spacing, disciplined type, few accents.
   The confidence comes from what is left out, not piled on.
3. **Hierarchy over volume.** Surface the headline, let the detail wait. A
   visitor should grasp the gist in seconds and be able to dig deeper by
   choice.
4. **Earn every element.** No decorative flourish, no card, no animation
   survives unless it does a job. If it could be removed without loss, remove
   it.
5. **Distinct, not loud.** The site should feel unmistakably crafted and
   memorable without raising its voice. Boldness through precision.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG target set. Hold to sensible defaults: legible contrast on the
dark navy palette, full keyboard navigation, honor `prefers-reduced-motion`,
and meaningful focus states. Do not constrain the design beyond these.
